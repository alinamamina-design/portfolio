By 2050, that number will more than double to 1.6 billion, meaning 1 in 6 people on the planet will be over 65. In Europe and North America, nearly 3 out of 4 older adults already live alone or with a partner only.

Solution

A mobile app that makes daily health monitoring effortless for older adults and transparent for their families. It connects to wearable devices, automatically collects key metrics, and sends data to relatives and doctors.

Top Features

01

Trend Tracking

Key vitals at a glance, in plain language, not raw numbers. Weekly and monthly charts to spot patterns early.

02

Medication Reminders

One-tap confirmation on phone or watch. Missed dose? The family gets notified.

03

Family Monitoring

A linked dashboard for relatives: vitals, meds, activity. Automatic alerts when something looks off.

What Went Wrong

Moderated usability testing with 12 participants from the target audience revealed critical problems:

01
Low accessibility
  • Text contrast only 2.4:1 (WCAG AA requires 4.5:1)
  • Touch targets at 32px, too small for older users
  • Icon-only navigation, users couldn't tell what each tab meant
02
Information overload
The main screen was overloaded with information. Users couldn't tell what needed attention and what was just background data.
05

Version 2

I rethought the entire app and focused on what actually matters to users:

01
The main screen shows only the essential widgets, reminders and notifications about important health changes
02
The second screen focuses on the most important health trends
03
The entire UI was rebuilt following WCAG AAA guidelines

Takeaways

As a team, we built an MVP of the product, but unfortunately never made it to the app store release.

Still, this project means a lot to me. I deeply explored the real problems older people face, and these are problems I genuinely care about.

I'm confident that one day I'll launch a product that makes life better for older adults. And to get there, you have to fail forward.
